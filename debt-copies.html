The Debt Copy Problem

Why the copies feel real — and why the board can never fully clear.

In Rummikub, tiles are fixed and finite. Every tile on the board at the start is still there at the end. No new tiles appear when you make a move. The game is a zero-sum redistribution of a known quantity.

Fractional reserve banking works differently. When a loan is made, a copy of the deposited money appears in the economy — real, spendable, indistinguishable from the original. The copy can be deposited, lent out, and copied again. The board fills with tiles that were never in the original box. Each one has a claim attached: it must eventually return to the bank that created it. Plus interest. And the interest tiles were never made.

Stage 1 of 6

What the analogy shows — and where it stops

What the Rummikub frame captures
The copies are genuinely real — you can't tell a lent dollar from a deposited one. This is not fraud at the level of any individual transaction. The problem is structural: every copy carries a claim back to its origin, and those claims, in aggregate, exceed the tiles on the board. The game cannot end with every player whole.
Where the analogy breaks down
In Rummikub, there is no concept of "interest." The return obligation in banking is what creates the gap — not the copying itself. A world of interest-free loans would still have copies, but the board could clear. The growth imperative comes specifically from charging interest on money that was created as debt.
The key distinction
Individual banks don't lend more than they take in. Each tile on this board was created by a legitimate transaction. The problem is not any single bank's behavior — it is that the system as a whole has created more claims than tiles. That gap is structural. It cannot be resolved by any individual actor acting more responsibly.
Why this matters beyond banking
If the only things that can receive a loan are things with collateral and a projected cash flow, then the only things that get copies made of them are already-valued assets. A wetland generates no cash flow. Care work generates no cash flow. The copy-making machine has a built-in filter — and what it filters out is exactly what the incomplete ledger fails to count.
