Stock vs Flow Systems

It's not the bucket. It's the rain.

A flow is a rate — rain falling now, a river moving now, solar energy arriving now. It exists only in the present. A stock is an accumulated quantity — water in a barrel, water in a reservoir, water in an aquifer. It persists between flows. A hybrid is a living system that does both: accumulates and actively maintains its own conditions for receiving more. Step through to see how each behaves when the rain stops.

Step 1 of 5
Flow: the water cycle
rain · evaporation · clouds · rain again
The water cycle is a flow system driven by solar energy. Water evaporates, forms clouds, falls as rain, and the cycle continues. The resource is the rate — what's falling right now.
Stock: a rain barrel
collects when it rains · depletes when used · empty is empty
A rain barrel collects what falls and holds it between rain events. You can use from it on dry days. But it doesn't refill itself — when it's empty, it's empty until the next rain.
Hybrid: a garden pond
stores water + actively maintains its own conditions
A pond with established plants and soil life is a hybrid. It stores water like a stock — but the biological activity also slows evaporation, filters the water, and builds conditions that help it retain more. Stress it enough and the living layer breaks down. Then it's just a hole.
At baseline, all three hold water. The difference only becomes visible when the rain stops.
Flow
A rate. Exists only while the source is active. Fails immediately when the source stops. Recovers immediately when it returns. You can't save yesterday's rain — you can only catch today's.

Other flows: solar radiation, wind, a river in a wet year, annual harvest from a healthy ecosystem, wages.
Stock
An accumulated quantity. Persists when the flow stops — for a while. Once depleted, it's gone until the flow refills it. Gives you a buffer. Does not maintain itself.

Other stocks: a reservoir, an aquifer, a savings account, coal and oil deposits, a pile of lumber, mineral ore.
Hybrid
A living system that accumulates AND actively maintains its own conditions. Forests pull moisture from air. Wetlands slow and hold runoff. Healthy soil absorbs ten times more rain than compacted soil. Degrade the biological layer and you lose both the stock and the self-maintenance.

Other hybrids: topsoil, a fishery, a forest, a wetland, a kelp forest, a dairy herd.
Flow — fails fast, recovers fast
Rainfall — arrives when conditions allow; stops when they don't
Solar energy — continuous but uncollectable at night
A spring — flows from groundwater pressure; stops in drought
Annual crop yield — renewed each season; zero between harvests
Wages — arrives while you work; stops when you don't
Wind — rate-dependent; zero when calm
Stock — fails slowly, recovers slowly
A rain barrel — holds what fell; depletes between rains
A reservoir — managed buffer in a larger flow system
Fossil groundwater — Ogallala Aquifer: 6,000 years to fill, decades to drain
Fossil fuels — 300 million years to accumulate; no recharge
Savings account — holds past wages; depletes without income
Old-growth forest — centuries of structure; can't be replanted into existence
Hybrid — fails at thresholds, often irreversible
Topsoil — 500 years to build one inch; biological structure can collapse in one season
A fishery — self-renewing if not overfished; collapses past a threshold
A wetland — filters water and stores it; degrade the plants and it becomes a mudflat
A forest watershed — trees pull moisture from air, slow runoff, recharge groundwater
A coral reef — living structure maintains itself; bleach it twice and it becomes rubble
A dairy herd — reproduces (flow) and exists (stock); overwork it and both collapse
