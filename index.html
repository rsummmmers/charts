How Money Actually Works

Four interactive models of fractional reserve banking and the interest problem

These models are designed to be used in sequence. Each one builds on the last. Start with #1 if you're new to the topic; jump to #3 or #4 to get directly to the structural argument.

1
The Money Multiplier
How $100 becomes $1,000 through the cascade of deposits and loans. The math is bounded — not infinite — but every dollar in the chain is someone's debt. Interactive slider shows how reserve requirements control the multiplier.
Reserve requirements · Deposit cascade · Bounded multiplication
→
2
The Debt Copy Problem
A Rummikub analogy for why the copies feel real — and why the board can never fully clear. Step through the game from first deposit to the unclearable interest gap.
Money creation · Claim chains · Why individual banks aren't the problem
→
3
The Interest Ratchet
Why the gap between money that exists and money that is owed can only grow. Each attempt to close the gap with new borrowing creates a slightly larger gap. Tracks the compounding over 7 cycles.
Structural growth imperative · Compound gap · The off-switch problem
→
4
The Interest Variable
Holds everything constant and changes one thing: whether loans carry interest. Toggle between interest-free and interest-bearing to see exactly where the structural problem enters.
A/B comparison · Isolates the interest mechanism · The specific design flaw
→

These models address the mechanics of money creation. They do not address what those mechanics filter out: care work, ecological infrastructure, and anything else that generates no cash flow and carries no collateral.
